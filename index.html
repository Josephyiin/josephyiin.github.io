    Research

    From the lab bench to the bedside

    I develop data-efficient and trustworthy AI methods for decisions that rest on small, noisy, multimodal real-world data.

    Responsible LLMs and agents for healthcare

    Post-training open LLMs (QLoRA, DPO, GRPO, adversarial alignment) to extract social determinants of health from clinical notes, with subgroup audits across race, sex, and payer. Engineering multimodal multi-agent systems that keep clinicians in control.

    Signals, notes, and EHR in one model

    Aligning smartwatch streams with continuous glucose traces to warn of hypoglycemia, an event with under 1% positive rate. A ClinicalBERT model with a gated multimodal unit that weighs notes and structured EHR per patient.

    Deep learning on small scientific image sets

    Lightweight, simple yet effective models for millimeter-scale corrosion: detection with self-supervised learning, segmentation of thin, low-contrast boundaries with scientist-infused learning, and lab-to-real transfer from accelerated indoor tests to outdoor exposure. Also a promptable GAN that generates aluminum alloy microstructures in seconds instead of weeks of casting trials.

    Learning from noisy and partial labels

    Comparing where humans and neural networks attend in text, and using human attention as supervision. Extending the same expert-in-the-loop idea to label denoising and LLM-assisted annotation.

    Currently exploring: multimodal agents, test-time training, sim-to-real transfer, self-improving loops, graph agents, causal ML, world models, positive-unlabeled learning, and closed-loop scientific discovery.
